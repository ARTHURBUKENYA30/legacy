Young athletes come first

Child safeguarding information

The academy wants young people to participate in sport in an environment that is respectful, inclusive and safe. This page sets out practical expectations for families and anyone involved in youth activities. The academy should approve and adapt these statements to match its actual procedures before treating them as a formal safeguarding policy.

Ask the academy to identify its designated safeguarding lead and an alternative contact. This page does not yet name a designated officer.

Our expectations

  • Every young athlete should be treated with dignity and without bullying, harassment, discrimination or humiliating punishment.
  • Coaches, volunteers and adults should maintain appropriate professional boundaries with young people.
  • Parents and guardians should receive clear information about activities, schedules, supervision and collection arrangements.
  • Concerns about a child's safety should be taken seriously, recorded appropriately and escalated to the right responsible person or relevant authorities where necessary.

Photography and stories

Obtain appropriate parent or guardian consent and the child's agreement where suitable before publishing identifiable photographs, videos, personal stories or results. Do not publish a child's private contact details, precise routine, sensitive personal information or location information that could put them at risk. Respect requests not to be photographed.

Communication and online conduct

Use transparent, appropriate communication channels for academy matters. Avoid secretive or inappropriate one-to-one contact with children. Do not post or share images of young athletes without permission, and report suspicious or harmful online behaviour.

Training, travel and events

Before activities or travel, families should be told the location, timing, responsible adults, supervision and emergency arrangements. The academy should document consent, collection arrangements and emergency contacts as appropriate for the activity.

How to report a concern

  1. If a child is in immediate danger, contact the appropriate local emergency or child-protection services.
  2. For an academy-related concern, email the address above and ask for the designated safeguarding lead. Share only the information necessary to explain the concern.
  3. If the concern involves the person who normally receives reports, request an alternative senior contact or contact the appropriate external child-protection authority.

What the academy should put in place

Before presenting this as a formal policy, the academy should designate a safeguarding lead and deputy, approve a written reporting procedure, define staff and volunteer conduct expectations, establish appropriate recruitment and training checks, maintain secure incident records, and review the policy regularly in line with applicable Ugandan requirements.

This is a public-facing starting point, not a claim that all safeguarding controls are already in place. The academy should review, approve and operationalise the procedures before publication as a formal policy.
